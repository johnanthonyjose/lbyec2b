I.Rationale and Coverage

A working program no longer shows, by itself, that the student who submitted it can analyse a problem and develop an algorithm. An audited exercise is therefore conducted differently: you implement it, and thereafter explain and defend that work under supervision — in a single sitting where the exercise allows, otherwise across the week.

In brief. Everything you claim about what you built, you shall write in the room.

These mechanics are standing and apply, without modification, to every exercise the faculty-in-charge nominates for audit. Only the panel above changes from one activity to the next. The weight of each audited activity is taken from the Machine Problems or Laboratory component, as stated in that panel.

A dry run shall be held once, before the first audited exercise of the term. It is not graded, but attendance is required, as the mechanics shall not be explained again for subsequent activities.

II.Definition of Terms

Audit Team
Six (6) students, fixed for the term, distinct from the Project Team.
Team Leader
The member who coordinates the team and reviews its evidence. Evaluated by the faculty-in-charge.
Plan
A one-page outline of your approach, written before coding and locked on submission.
Divergence Log
What changed between your Plan and your finished program, and why. In Mode B it cites your commits; in Mode A it is a short note written immediately after coding.
Modification
A written change to a constraint of the problem — not merely to a value within it.
Response
Your supervised written answer to a Modification, concerning your own program.

III.Audit Teams

  1. Teams of six (6) shall be posted on Canvas before the dry run and shall stand for the whole term, across every audited exercise. No Audit Team shall reproduce an existing Project Team.
  2. Each team shall have one (1) Team Leader and one (1) Deputy, designated by the faculty-in-charge.
  3. Assignments follow a ring published with the team lists and rotated for each audited exercise. Reciprocal pairings are not permitted: the member who formulates your Modification is not the member for whom you formulate one.
  4. Each member shall formulate one (1) Modification, receive two (2) — one from a teammate and one from the faculty-in-charge — evaluate two (2) members, and be evaluated by two (2).

IV.Mechanics and Schedule

The audit runs in one of two modes. The mode for this activity is stated in the panel on the first page. Everything else in these mechanics is identical in both.

Mode A — single sitting

For laboratory exercises small enough to be implemented within the session. The implementation is itself written in the room, under observation.

TimeWhat you do — one continuous session, about two (2) hours
10 minWrite the Plan, then lock it. Bullets: your decomposition, your data structures, and which parts you expect to be difficult.

5 minNote what changed from your Plan, and why.

12 minFormulate one (1) Modification of their exercise, and state in a single sentence which part of their design you expect it to break. That sentence is what is graded.

If your program does not work. In Mode A you answer the Modifications against the design you actually attempted, working or not. A program left incomplete within the hour does not forfeit the Responses, which carry the larger weight.

Mode B — multi-day

For machine problems too large for one sitting. Implementation is taken home, and the commit history is read against the Plan in its place. Days are counted from release; calendar dates are in the panel on the first page.

Each Response shall address, in order

  1. What breaks in your program under the Modification, and why;
  2. The minimum change that would address it;
  3. The revised algorithm, as pseudo-code; and
  4. One (1) thing that could still go wrong thereafter.
On the Plan. A prediction of difficulty that proves mistaken is not penalised. A mistaken prediction later accounted for in your Divergence Log is credited more highly than a cautious one that says nothing. Write what you honestly expect.

A valid Modification alters a constraint: an assumption about input validity, a limit on scale or memory, the shape or arrival order of the data, a new failure condition, or the removal of an assumption the original relied upon. Changing a literal, renaming an identifier, or requiring extra output shall not be credited.

Each criterion is rated on the four-point scale used elsewhere in this course — Exemplary, Satisfactory, Developing, Beginning.

Evidence examined

Published in advance, so that no student is assessed on anything he or she was not informed of: the Plan and the account of what changed; the program; the Modification you formulated; your two Responses; the observations of the two members who evaluated you; and, in Mode B only, the commit history.

Where the commit history is examined, it is used only to raise a question for clarification, and never to set a mark directly. There is nothing to be gained by inflating commits.

VI.Conduct of the Session

  • Permitted. Your own repository, read-only; the machine problem; writing materials. Answers may be written on paper.
  • Not permitted. Internet access, any AI assistant, mobile telephones, or any file other than your own repository.
  • Form. Pseudo-code, flowcharts, diagrams and bullet fragments are all accepted.
  • Not graded. Handwriting, spelling and command of English. Only the reasoning is assessed. You may instead record your Response aloud, within the same time limit, without giving a reason.

VII.Artificial Intelligence

The principle applied. This activity is not designed to detect the use of AI. It is designed so that the question seldom needs to be asked: the portions carrying the marks are written without internet access, about a program only you have built, answering a change of which you had no notice. Where the work is not one's own, there is very little to say — and no allegation is needed for that to become apparent.
  1. The course policy and the Student Handbook provisions on academic honesty apply in full.
  2. Any member may raise a concern, which must cite something specific and verifiable. A general impression shall not be entertained.
  3. No incentive or credit of any kind attaches to raising a concern.
  4. A concern is resolved by a conversation of about five (5) minutes with the faculty-in-charge regarding your own code. Ordinarily the matter ends there.

VIII.Other Provisions

  1. Absence. A student unable to attend shall re-sit. Absence shall not by itself result in a grade of zero (0.0). Where no re-sit is taken, the faculty-in-charge shall record insufficient evidence and no credit is given for the supervised portions.
  2. Default of a classmate. Where no Modification is formulated for you, the faculty-in-charge shall issue one. No student is prejudiced by another's default.
  3. Failure to perform a role. Failure to formulate an assigned Modification or accomplish an assigned evaluation results in no credit for that component, and does not affect the member who was to receive it.
  4. Marks. All marks are determined by the faculty-in-charge. Peer evaluations are received as evidence and are subject to review; no student assigns a grade to another student.
  5. Reconsideration. May be sought within one (1) week of release, during consultation hours. The evidence relied upon shall be shown to the student.

IX.Guide for Team Leaders

Addressed to Team Leaders and Deputies, and published to the whole class so that every member knows what the Team Leader may and may not do.

The role

You are not a grader. You ensure the evidence your team produces is complete and properly supported before it reaches the faculty-in-charge. Your performance in that role is assessed under LO6. You are audited on your own machine problem like everyone else; the Deputy reviews your submissions.

Duties

  1. Before Day 0. Confirm every member knows the schedule and their ring assignment.
  2. Day 4. Confirm every member has pushed. Report any who have not the same day — not on Day 5, when it is too late to reassign a partner.
  3. Day 5. Confirm attendance at the start; report absences immediately.
  4. Days 6–7. Review the ten (10) evaluation forms for evidence quality and return any that fall short.
  5. Day 7. Submit the one-page team summary: that the review was done, which forms were returned and whether corrected, and anything the faculty-in-charge should know.

Reviewing a form

You check one thing: whether each rating cites specific, checkable evidence — not whether you agree with it. A harsh rating with evidence is forwarded; a generous rating without evidence is returned.

Return. “He understood the problem well and his response was good.” Nothing here can be verified by anyone who was not present.

Forward. “His Response identified that the reused buffer would be overwritten once rows arrived out of order, and pointed to the loop where this occurs.” Specific, and checkable against the Response itself.

You must not

  • Assign, adjust, negotiate or predict any mark. Any assurance you give will not bind the faculty-in-charge.
  • Determine whether a member used AI. Record the specific observation in your summary and leave it there.
  • Perform any part of a member's work, however far behind they are. Report it instead.
  • Amend a rating. You may return a form; you may not change one.
  • Withhold an unfavourable observation to protect the team. Your own mark depends on the integrity of the review.

A short, exact summary naming one real difficulty is worth considerably more than a long one reporting that all was well.

X.Annex — Setting Up an Audited Exercise

For the faculty-in-charge. Nothing in Sections I to IX changes between activities; only the following are prepared afresh for each one.

To prepare

  1. Complete the Activity Details panel on the first page and post the document to Canvas.
  2. Write the Common Modification — one probe the whole class answers against their own programs. It shall be generic in wording yet answerable only with reference to the student's own design, and shall alter a constraint. This is what makes results comparable across the class, so it is prepared before release, not on the day.
  3. Prepare two (2) default Modifications for members left without one through a classmate's default.
  4. Rotate the ring so that no pairing repeats from a previous audited exercise, and post it with the Activity Details.
  5. Reserve the time. Mode A: one continuous session of about two (2) hours. Mode B: fifteen (15) minutes at release and a protected sixty-minute (60) session on Day 5.

Choosing a suitable exercise

Not every exercise can be audited. A suitable one has constraints that can be altered — assumptions about input validity, scale, data shape or arrival order — so that a Modification has something real to attack. Exercises with a single prescribed output and no assumptions to remove will not yield a usable probe.

Choosing the mode

Mode A wherever the exercise fits the hour. Observing the implementation directly is stronger evidence than inferring it from a commit history afterwards, and the whole activity closes in a single session. Mode A suits laboratory exercises and smaller machine problems.

Mode B only where the exercise genuinely cannot be completed in one sitting. The commit history then stands in for the observation that Mode A gets directly. Accept the weaker evidence only in exchange for the larger problem — Mode B is the concession, not the default.

Note the trade in what is measured. Mode A rewards fluency and penalises the student who reasons slowly; Mode B allows the deliberation that analysing a substantial problem requires. Where both are used in a term, the two together give a fuller picture than either alone.

Frequency

Any number of exercises may be audited in a term. Where more than one is audited, the Audit Teams stand and only the ring rotates; the dry run is held once, before the first.

XI.Queries

Clarifications may be raised during consultation hours or through Canvas. Kindly raise any question before the dry run, so that the mechanics are settled well in advance of the first audited activity.
