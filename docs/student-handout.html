The exercise audit

Anyone can submit code that works. This is where you show that the reasoning behind yours is yours.

What happens, and when

You build the exercise at home, in your own time. Everything you claim about what you built, you write in the room.

Two-thirds of this component is written in the room, on your own program. That is deliberate: those are the parts nobody else can write for you.

What gets read

Published in advance on purpose. Nothing here is looked at that you weren't told about.

  • Your plan, and your divergence log against it.
  • Your commit history — how the work was spread out, and where the rework actually went.
  • Your submitted program.
  • The modification you wrote, and the sentence saying what you expected it to break.
  • Your two responses.
  • What two people in your pod observed about your work.

Your commit history is only ever used to raise a question, never to set a mark directly. There's nothing to gain from padding it.

On AI

The short version

This isn't built to catch you. It's built so that the question doesn't arise: the parts that carry the marks are written in a room with no internet, about a program that only you built, answering a change nobody warned you about. If the work isn't yours, there won't be much to say — and no accusation is needed for that to show.

Anyone can raise a concern about a submission, including about your own. It costs nothing, earns nothing, and must point at something specific and checkable. It is settled by a five-minute conversation about your code with the instructor, which is normally the end of it.

In the room

You may have
Your own repository, open and read-only. Paper, if you prefer it.
You may not have
The internet, any assistant, or your phone.
Form of the answer
Pseudocode, diagrams and bullet fragments are all accepted. Write in whichever gets the reasoning across fastest.
Not graded
Handwriting, spelling, and the quality of your English. Only the reasoning is marked. Ask to answer aloud into a recorder instead if that suits you better — no justification needed.

Pods, and what to do if something goes wrong

Your pod
Six students, fixed for the term, with a leader and a deputy. Your pod is separate from your project team.
The leader's job
Check that everyone's work is in before each session, and send back any evaluation that doesn't cite real evidence. The leader is evaluated by the instructor, not by you — and is audited on their own exercise exactly like everyone else.
You miss the session
You re-sit it. A missed session is not a zero.
Nobody wrote you a modification
The instructor issues one. You are never penalised for someone else's default.
Your file doesn't add up
You're asked for five minutes on your own code. That conversation usually resolves it.

Why it's built this way

Writing your reasoning down, in a room, under time, about a system you built — a design review, an incident write-up, a code review — is the ordinary professional condition. It is worth being good at for its own sake. The fact that it is also difficult to outsource is the smaller half of the reason it's here.
