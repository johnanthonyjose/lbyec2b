I.Rationale

A working program, taken by itself, no longer demonstrates that the student who submitted it can analyse a problem, develop an algorithm, and account for the decisions taken along the way. These abilities are what Learning Outcomes 1 to 4 require, and they are what this activity is designed to make observable.

The activity is therefore structured as an audit. The student implements the machine problem in his or her own time, and thereafter explains and defends that work under supervision. The faculty-in-charge forms a judgment from the whole set of evidence gathered, and not from the submitted program alone.

In brief

You shall build the exercise at home. Everything you claim about what you built, you shall write in the room.

II.Coverage and Weight

  1. This activity shall cover the machine problem assigned in Week 6, Elementary Matrix Operations, and shall be assessed against Learning Outcomes 1 to 4.
  2. The activity shall constitute twenty-five percent (25%) of the final grade, in lieu of the corresponding portion of the Machine Problems component.
  3. A dry run shall be conducted in Week 3. The dry run shall not be graded. Attendance during the dry run is nonetheless required, as the mechanics shall not be explained again in Week 6.
  4. This activity is separate from, and does not replace, the Practical Examination in Week 12 nor the Term Project.

III.Definition of Terms

For purposes of these mechanics, the following terms shall be understood as defined hereunder.

Audit Team
A group of six (6) students, fixed for the duration of the term, within which the activity is conducted. The Audit Team is distinct from the Project Team.
Team Leader
The member designated to coordinate the Audit Team and to review the completeness and quality of the evidence it produces. The Team Leader is evaluated by the faculty-in-charge.
Deputy
The member designated to assume the duties of the Team Leader in the latter's absence, and to review the Team Leader's own submissions.
Plan
A written outline, prepared before coding begins, of how the student intends to approach the machine problem. Once submitted, the Plan is locked and may no longer be revised.
Divergence Log
A written account of what changed between the Plan and the finished program, and why, with reference to the student's own commits.
Modification
A written change to the conditions of a machine problem, formulated by one student and answered by another. A Modification shall alter a constraint of the problem and not merely a value within it.
Peer Modification
The Modification formulated by an assigned member of the student's Audit Team.
Common Modification
A single Modification prepared by the faculty-in-charge and answered by the entire class, so that one comparable measure is obtained for every student.
Response
The student's written answer to a Modification, prepared under supervision and pertaining to his or her own submitted program.
Supervised Session
A timed laboratory period, conducted without internet access, during which the Modification, the Divergence Log, and the Responses are written.

IV.Composition of the Audit Teams

  1. The class shall be divided into Audit Teams of six (6) members each. Assignment to teams shall be made by the faculty-in-charge and shall be posted on Canvas before Week 3.
  2. No Audit Team shall reproduce an existing Project Team. This is intended to keep the two sets of working relationships independent of each other.
  3. Each Audit Team shall have one (1) Team Leader and one (1) Deputy, both designated by the faculty-in-charge. The duties of the Team Leader are set out in Section XI.
  4. Within each Audit Team, the assignment of who formulates a Modification for whom, and who evaluates whom, shall follow a ring published together with the team lists. Reciprocal pairings are not permitted: the member who formulates your Modification is not the member for whom you formulate one.
  5. Each member shall, in the course of the activity, formulate one (1) Peer Modification, receive one (1) Peer Modification and one (1) Common Modification, evaluate two (2) other members, and be evaluated by two (2) other members.

V.Mechanics

A. Phase 1 — The Plan (Day 0, fifteen (15) minutes, supervised)

  1. The machine problem shall be released during the regular session. Within the same session, each student shall be given fifteen (15) minutes to prepare the Plan.
  2. The Plan shall not exceed one (1) page and shall be written in bullet form. It shall state how the student intends to decompose the problem, what data structures are to be used, and which parts the student expects to be difficult.
  3. The Plan shall be submitted and locked at the end of the fifteen (15) minutes.
Please take note

A prediction of difficulty that later turns out to be mistaken is not penalised. On the contrary, a mistaken prediction that is afterwards accounted for in the Divergence Log is credited more highly than a cautious prediction that says nothing. You are advised to write what you honestly expect, and not what appears safe.

B. Phase 2 — Implementation (Days 1 to 4, at home)

  1. The student shall implement the machine problem individually and at his or her own pace. This phase is not timed.
  2. Work shall be committed to the student's own repository progressively, as the implementation proceeds, and not in a single commit upon completion.
  3. The completed program shall be pushed on or before the end of Day 4. The submission is governed by the late-submission policy in the course syllabus.

C. Phase 3 — The Supervised Session (Day 5, sixty (60) minutes)

  1. The session shall be conducted in one (1) sitting of sixty (60) minutes, in four (4) parts and in the following order:
    • Eight (8) minutes. Read the program of the member assigned to you.
    • Twelve (12) minutes. Formulate one (1) Peer Modification of that member's exercise, and state in a single sentence which part of their design you expect it to break. That sentence is the portion that is graded.
    • Ten (10) minutes. Write your Divergence Log, referring to your own commit history. The Modifications are distributed during this interval.
    • Thirty (30) minutes. Answer the two (2) Modifications you have received — one from a member of your Audit Team and one from the faculty-in-charge — at fifteen (15) minutes each.
  2. Each Response shall address the following four (4) points, in order:
    • What breaks in your program under the Modification, and why;
    • The minimum change that would address it;
    • The revised algorithm, expressed as pseudo-code; and
    • One (1) thing that could still go wrong thereafter.
  3. A Modification shall alter a constraint of the problem, such as an assumption regarding the validity of the input, a limit on scale or memory, the shape or order of arrival of the data, an additional failure condition to be handled, or the removal of an assumption the original solution relied upon. Merely changing a literal value, renaming an identifier, or requiring an additional line of output shall not be credited.

D. Phase 4 — Evaluations (Days 5 to 7, at home)

  1. Each student shall accomplish two (2) evaluation forms, covering the two (2) members assigned under Section IV, and shall submit the same through Canvas on or before Day 7.
  2. Every rating shall be supported by one (1) sentence citing specific evidence from the work being evaluated. A rating submitted without supporting evidence shall be returned by the Team Leader for completion.
  3. The evaluations are submitted to the faculty-in-charge as evidence. No student assigns a grade to another student. All marks are determined by the faculty-in-charge.

Sixty-seven percent (67%) of the activity is accordingly produced under supervision. This proportion is deliberate.

Evidence considered by the faculty-in-charge

The following shall be examined. It is published in advance so that no student is assessed on anything he or she was not informed of.

  1. The Plan, and the Divergence Log read against it;
  2. The commit history — the manner in which the work was distributed over the period, and where the rework in fact occurred;
  3. The submitted program;
  4. The Peer Modification formulated, together with the accompanying sentence;
  5. The two (2) Responses; and
  6. The observations of the two (2) members who evaluated the work.
Regarding the commit history

The commit history is used solely to raise a question for clarification. It shall never be used to determine a mark directly. There is accordingly nothing to be gained by inflating the number of commits.

VIII.Conduct of the Supervised Session

  1. Permitted. Your own repository, opened in read-only mode; the printed or on-screen copy of the machine problem; and writing materials. Answers may be written on paper if preferred.
  2. Not permitted. Internet access of any kind; any artificial intelligence assistant; mobile telephones, which shall be kept away for the duration of the session; and any file other than your own repository.
  3. Form of the answer. Pseudo-code, flowcharts, diagrams, and bullet fragments are all acceptable. Students are encouraged to use whichever conveys the reasoning most directly.
  4. Not graded. Handwriting, spelling, and command of English shall not be graded. Only the reasoning shall be assessed.
  5. Any student who prefers to give the Response orally may do so, by recording it in the room within the same time limit. No justification need be given for such a request.

IX.Policy on Artificial Intelligence

The principle applied

This activity is not designed to detect the use of artificial intelligence. It is designed so that the question seldom needs to be asked. The portions that carry the marks are written in a room without internet access, concerning a program that only you have built, and in answer to a change of which you had no advance notice. Where the work is not one's own, there is very little to say — and no allegation is required for that to become apparent.

  1. The prevailing policy of the course and the provisions of the Student Handbook on academic honesty shall apply in full to all phases of this activity.
  2. Any member may raise a concern regarding any submission. A concern must cite something specific and verifiable — for instance, an inconsistency between the Divergence Log and the commit history. A general impression shall not be entertained.
  3. No incentive, credit, or advantage of any kind attaches to the raising of a concern. A concern is an observation submitted to the faculty-in-charge and nothing further.
  4. A concern shall be resolved by a conversation of approximately five (5) minutes between the student and the faculty-in-charge regarding the student's own code. In the ordinary case, the matter ends there. Where it does not, the matter shall be dealt with under the Student Handbook and not under these mechanics.

X.Other Provisions

  1. Re-sit. Any student may request one (1) re-sit of the Supervised Session, to be held on or before Week 8. No reason need be given. The request shall be made through Canvas.
  2. Absence. A student who is unable to attend the Supervised Session shall re-sit it. Absence shall not by itself result in a grade of zero (0.0). Where no re-sit is taken, the faculty-in-charge shall record that there is insufficient evidence upon which to assess the student, and no credit shall be given for the supervised portions.
  3. Default of a classmate. Where the member assigned to formulate your Peer Modification fails to do so, the faculty-in-charge shall issue one in its place. No student shall be prejudiced by the default of another.
  4. Failure to perform an assigned role. Failure to formulate an assigned Modification, or to accomplish an assigned evaluation, shall result in no credit for that component for the member concerned, and shall not affect the student who was to receive it.
  5. Final determination of marks. All marks under this activity are determined by the faculty-in-charge. Peer evaluations are received as evidence and are subject to review; they do not of themselves constitute a grade.
  6. Reconsideration. A student who wishes to seek reconsideration of a mark may do so within one (1) week of its release, during consultation hours. The evidence considered in arriving at the mark shall be shown to the student.

XI.Guide for Team Leaders

This section is addressed to the designated Team Leaders and Deputies. It forms part of these mechanics and is published to the whole class, so that every member knows what the Team Leader is and is not authorised to do.

A. What the role is

You are not a grader. You are the member who ensures that the evidence your team produces is complete and properly supported before it reaches the faculty-in-charge. Your own performance in that role is evaluated by the faculty-in-charge and forms part of your mark under Learning Outcome 6. You are likewise audited on your own machine problem, in exactly the same manner as every other member; the Deputy reviews your submissions.

B. Duties, by phase

  1. Before Day 0. Confirm that every member knows the schedule, the venue, and the ring assignment. Report any member who has not been assigned a partner.
  2. On Day 4. Confirm that every member has pushed a program. Where a member has not, inform the faculty-in-charge the same day — not on Day 5, when it is too late for a partner to be reassigned.
  3. On Day 5. Confirm attendance at the start of the Supervised Session and report any absence immediately, so that a re-sit may be arranged and the affected partner given the Common Modification in place of the Peer Modification.
  4. On Days 6 to 7. Review the ten (10) evaluation forms produced by your team for evidence quality, and return any that fall short. Guidance follows in Section D.
  5. On Day 7. Submit the team summary — one (1) page, on the prescribed form, stating that the review was carried out, listing any evaluations returned and whether they were corrected, and noting any matter the faculty-in-charge ought to be aware of.

C. Your checklist for the week

  • All six (6) members know the schedule and their ring assignment.
  • All six (6) Plans were submitted and locked on Day 0.
  • All six (6) programs were pushed by Day 4; exceptions reported the same day.
  • Attendance on Day 5 confirmed; absences reported before the session began.
  • All ten (10) evaluation forms received; those lacking cited evidence returned and corrected.
  • Team summary submitted on Day 7.
  • Your own artefacts reviewed by the Deputy.

D. Reviewing an evaluation form

You are reviewing one thing only: whether each rating cites specific evidence that can be checked. You are not reviewing whether you agree with the rating. A harsh rating that cites evidence is acceptable and shall be forwarded; a generous rating that cites nothing is not, and shall be returned.

Return this
He understood the problem well and his response was good.

No evidence is cited. Nothing here can be verified by anyone who was not present.

Forward this
His Response identified that the reused buffer would be overwritten once the rows arrived out of order, and he pointed to the specific loop where this occurs.

Specific, and capable of being checked against the Response itself.

When returning a form, state which rating lacks evidence and what is required. Give the member until the end of Day 7. Where a member declines to correct it, forward the form as submitted and note the matter in your summary; it is not for you to resolve.

E. What you must not do

  • Do not assign, adjust, negotiate, or predict any mark. You have no such authority, and any assurance you give on the matter will not bind the faculty-in-charge.
  • Do not determine whether a member has used artificial intelligence. Where something appears inconsistent, record the specific observation in your summary and leave the matter there.
  • Do not perform any part of a member's work, however far behind that member may be. Report it instead.
  • Do not change a member's rating of another member. You may return a form; you may not amend one.
  • Do not withhold an unfavourable observation in order to protect the team. Your own mark depends on the integrity of the review, not on the standing of your members.

F. How your own performance is assessed

The faculty-in-charge shall assess the team summary and the quality of the evaluations your team submitted after your review — specifically, whether the forms that reached the faculty-in-charge cite genuine evidence, and whether the matters you raised were accurate and proportionate. A short, exact summary that names one real difficulty is worth considerably more than a long one that reports that all was well.

XII.Queries

Clarifications regarding these mechanics may be raised with the faculty-in-charge during consultation hours, or through the course page on Canvas. Students are encouraged to raise any question before the dry run in Week 3, so that the mechanics are settled well in advance of the graded activity in Week 6.
